## Priority 3

- **API panel examples always end in `.pdf`**, and the panel names `docs/api` as plain text, not a link.
- **`not_equals` keeps items whose field is missing.** Documented, but may surprise.
- **Scheduler lines in the Run feed** are made in the browser. They vanish on reload and in replay, and count waiting calls of all jobs.
- **The interactive lane tile on Run** counts interactive calls across all models, so a bulk run can show "Going first" for a model it does not use.
- **Queue positions on Activity** are worked out in the browser from the latest 80 jobs, across models, so they are approximate.

- **A job that fails while its calls are being admitted can meet a database deadlock**, which Postgres resolves by cancelling one side; the recording is then tried again. Admission now skips locked calls, which removes the case that was found. Listed so a reviewer looks for others.

### High

- **R1** (confirmed, functional; worker and planner) `doc_engine/gateway.py:175`  
  Failures in the middle of a streamed call are classified 'permanent', so the job fails instead of retrying. An SSE `error` event (overloaded_error, api_error, rate_limit_error) is raised by the SDK as APIStatusError carrying the stream's HTTP status 200, which is not in TRANSIENT_STATUS. A dropped connection mid-stream raises a raw httpx2 ReadError/RemoteProtocolError that the SDK does not wrap, and it falls into `except Exception` -> permanent.  
  *How it shows:* A flow ends in a Reduce step (1 task, so stream=True in auto mode). After the map stage has spent many calls, the gateway sends `event: error {type: overloaded_error}` 200 s into the streamed reply, or a proxy resets the connection. execute_call sets outcome='permanent' (http_status 200). _record goes to the else branch and fail_job fails the job with 'Request failed', where the non-streamed path would have retried a 529/5xx/connection error. A mid-stream rate_limit_error also fails the job and never sets the 429 hold.  
  *Suggested fix:* In the streaming branch, map APIStatusError raised after the stream opened by its body error type (overloaded_error/api_error -> transient, rate_limit_error -> 429), and catch httpx2.TransportError (or anthropic.APIConnectionError plus raw httpx2 errors) as transient.

### Medium

- **R2** (confirmed, functional; scheduler in the database) `sql/001_schema.sql:509`  
  de_release corrects in_level by subtracting the call's admission estimate from the current level, even when that estimate has already drained away, so it removes input that other calls charged more recently.  
  *How it shows:* Model with itpm 60,000 (in_capacity 57,000, drains 1,000/s). Call A is admitted at t=0 with input_estimate 40,000 and runs for 70 s, so by t=40 in_level has drained to 0. At t=69 call B (est 40,000) is admitted (in_level 40,000) and C (est 40,000) is blocked. At t=70 A ends with a gateway timeout (transient, worker passes counted_in 0): in_level = greatest(0, 39,000 + 0 - 40,000) = 0, and C is admitted at once. The gateway receives 80,000 input tokens within about a second against a 60,000/min limit and rejects with 429. The same happens on an ok reply whose actual counted input is below its estimate (est 40,000, actual 25,000 leaves the level 15,000 too low). The ledger lets more input through than in_capacity.  
  *Suggested fix:* Remove only the part of the estimate that has not drained yet. For example, keep the estimate's remaining undrained amount on the reservation, or clamp the correction to at most the level built up since r.admitted_at.
- **R3** (confirmed, functional; scheduler in the database) `doc_engine/worker.py:481`  
  _return_to_queue and de_reap (sql line 548) set a running task back to 'ready' without checking the job's state, and de_admit (sql line 330) never checks it either, so calls of cancelled or failed jobs are sent to the gateway again.  
  *How it shows:* A user cancels a 300-page bulk job while 128 of its calls are in flight. Before the replies arrive the app is redeployed (clean stop), or the worker dies. Each in-flight task goes back to 'ready' with its original priority and submission time. After restart, de_admit sends all 128 to the gateway, spending money and output quota ahead of later live jobs, and only then does _record mark them 'cancelled'.  
  *Suggested fix:* In _return_to_queue and de_reap, set the task to 'cancelled' (or skip requeueing it) when its job is not in queued/running, or have de_admit skip ready tasks whose job is not live.
- **R4** (confirmed, functional; scheduler in the database) `doc_engine/planner.py:259`  
  An interactive job can attach its calls to another, bulk job's warm-up that is 'running'. If that warm-up is then sent back to 'ready' (429, transient retry or reap), it queues again at bulk priority, and the interactive job's calls wait behind the whole bulk backlog.  
  *How it shows:* Bulk job J0 (other prompt, same model) has 2,000 calls queued. Bulk job J1 of flow F has its warm-up W running when an interactive job J2 of F is planned, so J2's calls get gate_task_id = W (state = 'running' satisfies the query). W gets a 429, and _record sets it to 'ready' with priority_rank 1 and J1's submitted_at. The model's head is now J0's bulk calls, and W, with all of J2's interactive calls waiting on it, is sent only after J0's 2,000 calls. This breaks the rule that interactive work goes before bulk.  
  *Suggested fix:* When a job attaches to a shared warm-up, raise the warm-up's (priority_rank, job_submitted_at) to the least of its waiters, or on requeue let the gated waiters run without it.
- **R5** (confirmed, functional; worker and planner) `doc_engine/worker.py:251`  
  _record_patiently waits only on psycopg.OperationalError/InterfaceError/OSError, but Db.run turns database failures into RuntimeError (Db.connect raises RuntimeError at db.py:48, and run raises RuntimeError after 3 attempts at db.py:102). The 'keep the reply and try again for about a minute' fix for issue 8 therefore never runs: a paid reply hits _run's generic handler and the job fails.  
  *How it shows:* Postgres (Lakebase) restarts or fails over and is gone for about 35 s while calls are in flight. A reply arrives, and _record -> db.tx -> run -> conn() -> connect() retries for about 24 s, then raises RuntimeError('could not connect to Postgres after retries'). _record_patiently does not catch it, so it reaches _run's `except Exception` (worker.py:227). That builds outcome='permanent' with 'worker error: RuntimeError ...' and calls _record again. The database is back by then, so the else branch runs planner.fail_job and the whole job fails, even though the model call succeeded. The same happens when any DB read in _call (the _job or document fetch) meets a database outage longer than the connect retries.  
  *Suggested fix:* Have Db.run/connect raise (or re-raise) psycopg.OperationalError, or catch RuntimeError from the DB layer in _record_patiently. In _run, do not turn a DB-layer failure into a 'permanent' call outcome; return the task to the queue instead.
- **R6** (plausible, functional; worker and planner) `doc_engine/gateway.py:156`  
  A streamed reply that ends without message_delta/message_stop is accepted as a successful, complete reply. get_final_message() returns the partial snapshot (the SDK does not check for message_stop), stop_reason is None, and _finish marks any non-empty text as outcome 'ok'.  
  *How it shows:* A long streamed Reduce call (text output) has its response body closed cleanly by the gateway or a proxy at its execution limit (about 597 s), after half the summary was streamed. No exception is raised. _finish sees stop_reason=None with non-empty text and sets outcome 'ok'. The task is marked done with the half-written text, the job finishes 'done', and truncated is False (stop_reason is not 'max_tokens'), so even the cut-off warning on table outputs is not shown.  
  *Suggested fix:* In _finish (or after get_final_message), treat stop_reason None (no message_delta seen) as a transient failure rather than success.
- **R7** (plausible, security; worker and planner) `doc_engine/worker.py:280`  
  Every call loads its document through DocumentCache.material -> _doc, which has no in-flight deduplication. When a burst of calls is admitted for a file not yet cached, each coroutine misses the cache and fetches the whole document (up to 200 MB bytea) from Postgres separately, holding up to pool_size copies (times 2 for row plus bytes) in memory and filling the 16-connection pool.  
  *How it shows:* A 180 MB scanned 300-page PDF is run through a Map step with window 1. The warm-up completes and release_gated makes all 300 calls ready, and the dispatcher admits about 50-128 in quick succession. Each _run reaches documents.py:247 with an empty cache, so 16 concurrent 180 MB fetches hold about 3-6 GB, followed by roughly 100 more full fetches. The process can be killed for memory, which reaps every in-flight call and sends them again (paid twice). Or the pool is held for long enough that the 5 s heartbeat cannot get a connection for 30 s, so the worker's own in-flight calls are reaped as if it were dead.  
  *Suggested fix:* Deduplicate concurrent loads per document_id (an asyncio.Future/Lock per id in DocumentCache._doc), and fetch only the needed pages, or at least cap concurrent full-document loads.
- **R8** (plausible, functional; worker and planner) `doc_engine/gateway.py:139`  
  With gateway.token_source: databricks_oauth, auth_headers() calls config.databricks_oauth_token() for every model call. That function builds a new WorkspaceClient() and calls oauth_token() synchronously on the asyncio event loop, with no caching between calls. Every call therefore makes blocking config resolution and token HTTP requests, and a failed token fetch is classified as a permanent call failure.  
  *How it shows:* On Databricks Apps, a 500-call bulk job runs with concurrency 128. Each admitted call blocks the shared web+worker event loop for the WorkspaceClient construction and an OIDC token request (about 100-500 ms each). Admission, heartbeats and all API requests stall, and throughput falls to a few calls per second. If the OIDC endpoint rate-limits the burst, the exception reaches execute_call's `except Exception` (gateway.py:189) as outcome 'permanent', and _record fails the job.  
  *Suggested fix:* Create one WorkspaceClient/token source per process, cache the token until shortly before expiry, and fetch or refresh it off the event loop (asyncio.to_thread). Treat a token-fetch error as transient, not permanent.
- **R9** (confirmed, security; public endpoint and files) `doc_engine/public_api.py:126`  
  Multipart uploads are not size-limited. `request.form()` (lines 126 and 190) uses Starlette's defaults, which write every file part to a temporary file of any size and keep up to 1000 text fields of 1 MB each in memory. `read_part` checks the 200 MB limit only after the whole request has been written to disk. Issue 25's fix (stop reading at 200 MB) therefore covers only the raw-body path.  
  *How it shows:* A caller POSTs multipart/form-data to /api/v1/files, or to /api/v1/flows/<any published flow>/jobs, with one `file` part that streams 40 GB (or never ends). Starlette writes all of it to the temporary directory before `read_part` runs. `part.size` passes 200 MB only after the disk is full. Other uploads and anything else in the container that needs temporary disk then fail. The caller gets a 413 only at the end, if ever. A second variant: 1000 text fields of 1 MB each hold about 1 GB of memory per request before any field is checked.  
  *Suggested fix:* Stream the multipart body yourself with a byte counter (e.g. wrap request.stream() and abort past MAX_FILE_BYTES times the number of sources plus a small allowance), or check Content-Length first and refuse when it is missing or too large; also pass small max_files/max_fields to request.form().
- **R10** (confirmed, security; public endpoint and files) `doc_engine/public_api.py:211`  
  The JSON body of the job-submit endpoint is read with `await request.json()` and no size limit, then fully parsed into memory. The same process also runs the worker.  
  *How it shows:* A caller sends POST /api/v1/flows/<flow>/jobs with Content-Type application/json and a 3 GB body, for example {"values": {"x": "AAAA..."}}. Starlette buffers the whole body (the chunks plus the joined copy), and json.loads then builds another multi-GB string. The process runs out of memory and is killed. The worker in the same process dies with it, so its in-flight calls are lost until the reaper returns them to the queue, and they are sent and paid for again. A body of 100k nested brackets raises RecursionError instead, which surfaces as a 500.  
  *Suggested fix:* Read the body with documents.read_body(request, limit=~1 MB) and json.loads the result, refusing larger bodies with 413; catch RecursionError as invalid JSON.
- **R11** (confirmed, security; public endpoint and files) `doc_engine/documents.py:70`  
  `prepare_image` decodes uploaded images at full size and relies on Pillow's default limit, which only warns below about 179 M pixels. It then makes several full-size copies (an exif_transpose copy, an RGBA convert copy, a new RGB image). All of this runs synchronously inside the async handler (store_document at line 210), on the event loop shared with the worker.  
  *How it shows:* A caller uploads a PNG of about 700 KB holding one solid colour at 13300x13300 RGBA (176.9 M pixels, under the 2x limit, so Pillow only warns). load() takes about 708 MB. exif_transpose returns a copy (+708 MB), convert('RGBA') copies again (+708 MB), and Image.new('RGB') adds 531 MB, so peak memory is about 2 GB. The LANCZOS thumbnail and PNG encode follow, and the event loop is blocked for several seconds. Meanwhile the UI, the public API and the worker's heartbeat, admission and reply recording all stop. A few such uploads sent alongside large bodies can run a 6 GB Databricks App container out of memory and take the worker down with it.  
  *Suggested fix:* Set Image.MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS to a modest value (e.g. 40 M) and turn DecompressionBombWarning into an error, use Image.draft()/reduce for JPEG, avoid the redundant copies, and run prepare() (and pypdf parsing / sha256) in asyncio.to_thread so the worker's loop is not blocked.
- **R12** (confirmed, functional; public endpoint and files) `doc_engine/public_api.py:163`  
  flow_key removes every character outside [0-9a-z], letters with accents and non-Latin scripts included. Source titles that validation (lower() only) considers different can therefore share one key. by_key then keeps only the last of them, and the other source can never be supplied through the API. The same function makes publish (api.py:320) refuse flow names written in non-Latin scripts.  
  *How it shows:* A flow has two sources titled 'Счёт' and 'Договор', or 'Invoice (EU)' and 'Invoice EU'. Validation passes (titles differ in lower()) and the flow is published. Both titles map to the same flow_key ('' in the first case), so by_key holds only one source node and both form fields fill that same source. Every submit answers 400 'A file is needed for: Счёт', so the flow cannot be run through /api/v1 at all. Separately, publishing a flow named 'Проверка договора' fails with 'Give the flow a name before publishing' although it has a name.  
  *Suggested fix:* Normalise with Unicode-aware rules (casefold + unicodedata NFKD, keep \w letters of any script), and make flows.validate refuse two sources (and publish refuse two flows) whose flow_key collides.
- **R13** (confirmed, functional; flows, values and estimates) `doc_engine/flows.py:371`  
  Values are resolved twice: once by the caller (api.submit, public_api.v1_submit, cli load) and again in planner.submit_job on the already-coerced dict. A JSON input whose value is a JSON string is decoded twice, so the job is refused or the value changes.  
  *How it shows:* A flow declares a JSON input "Currency" with default '"EUR"' as typed in the builder textarea, or a caller sends values {"Currency": "\"EUR\""}. The first resolve_values returns the Python string EUR. submit_job (planner.py:89) calls resolve_values again, coerce_value runs json.loads("EUR"), and every run is refused with 'Input "Currency" is not valid JSON.', from the builder, from Activity and from /api/v1. The default passes validation, so the flow is published and can never run. Variant: a JSON value '"true"' becomes the string true, then the boolean True, and the prompt receives "yes".  
  *Suggested fix:* Do not coerce twice. Either pass the already-resolved values into submit_job with a flag to skip resolve_values, or make coerce_value for json leave a value alone when it is not a str that came from the caller (for example, resolve once in submit_job only).
- **R14** (confirmed, functional; flows, values and estimates) `doc_engine/flows.py:258`  
  A filter rule 'in {{Name}}' that uses a JSON list input never matches. apply_values writes the list as JSON text, and rule_matches splits that text on commas, so the options keep their brackets and quotes.  
  *How it shows:* A flow has a JSON input Types = ["invoice", "receipt"] and the filter rule page_type in {{Types}}. After apply_values the rule value is the string '["invoice", "receipt"]'. rule_matches splits it into '["invoice"' and ' "receipt"]', and norm keeps the brackets and quotes, so a page classified 'invoice' is dropped. Every item is filtered out, the extract step makes 0 calls, and the job finishes 'done' with an empty table and no error. Enum values that contain a comma (for example 'Reactive repair, electrical') also can never be matched with 'in'.  
  *Suggested fix:* In rule_matches for 'in', try json.loads on a string target first and use the list if it decodes to one. Alternatively, have apply_values substitute a list value as a list when the whole rule value is a single placeholder.
- **R15** (confirmed, functional; flows, values and estimates) `doc_engine/flows.py:349`  
  A number input drops every comma before parsing, so a decimal comma becomes a value ten or more times too large and is accepted without any error.  
  *How it shows:* A caller of /api/v1/flows/check-an-invoice-against-its-order/jobs sends the multipart field 'Price tolerance' = '0,5' (multipart values are always strings), or runs `load --value "Price tolerance=0,5"`. coerce_value turns it into 5, the prompt says 'Price tolerance: 5', and unit-price differences of up to 5 are reported as agreeing.   The draft autosave is a 700 ms timer that the effect cleanup cancels when the builder unmounts. A failed save sets 'Not saved' and is never retried. Edits made just before leaving the page, or before a failed PUT, are lost without a warning.

## Feature ideas, not started

Not issues. Kept here so they are not lost. None is to be started until the engine works end to end on the
real gateway and on Databricks Apps.

### A flow used as a step in another flow

Raised by the owner on 2026-11-18. A small flow such as "extract text from image" could be used inside other
flows, so its prompt, model, input and output settings are set up once.

- **Why it is worth doing:** one definition to maintain, and a fix reaches every flow that uses it.
- **Questions to settle first:**
  - Which version runs: the one published when the outer flow was saved, or whatever is published at run time.
  - How the inner flow's files and value inputs are filled from the outer flow.
  - Which of the inner flow's outputs becomes the block's output, and how page references pass through.
  - Refusing loops (a flow that reaches itself).
  - The cost estimate and the Run screen for a flow inside a flow.
- **The scheduler is not affected:** the inner flow's calls are tasks in the same queue under the same quota.
- **A smaller alternative:** saved steps (a step with its settings kept in the library and dropped into any
  flow). It covers "do not set this up again" with much less work, but it copies: a later fix does not reach
  flows already using it.

### A top priority level, set on the flow

Raised by the owner on 2026-11-18. Certain flows should go ahead of everything else that is waiting.

- **Today:** two levels per job, interactive and bulk. The queue sorts by a priority number, so a third level
  is a small change.
- **What to add:** a priority setting on the flow (Top, Interactive, Bulk) that its jobs inherit, and a rule
  that a caller of the endpoint cannot ask for more than the flow allows.
- **It jumps the line; it does not interrupt.** Calls already sent finish; the top job gets the next free
  capacity.
- **Questions to settle first:**
  - Starvation: a large top-priority load stops everything else. Keep the level for small jobs, or cap the
    share of the quota it may take.
  - Who may set it. This depends on sign-in and job owners (issues 15 and 26).
- **Not needed:** a way to lower a job's priority. Marking it bulk already does that.

### One day: a smart advisor agent

Raised by the owner on 2026-11-18 as a far-off idea. An agent that reads the stored record of flows, jobs and
calls and suggests improvements. A person accepts or ignores each suggestion.

- **Examples:** a large reference sent with many calls that could be summarized first; a step on an expensive
  model whose outputs a cheaper model could produce; the same step repeated across flows; a weekly report in
  plain language of what ran, what it cost and what failed.
- **It advises; it does not schedule.** Admission stays exact arithmetic in the database, so it is fast and
  every decision can be explained.
- **Do first, without an agent:** warnings from plain counts, such as "this step was cut off in 30% of calls,
  raise Max output tokens" and "this step uses a fifth of its Max output tokens".
